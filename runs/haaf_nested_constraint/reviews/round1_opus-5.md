1. **[S1] [docs/paper-sie-jmp/05-case-C-nested-constraints.md:104-105; also :93-95, :102; Notes/DECISIONS.md:5980-5981]** "LOO changes because the two Bayesian fits use different support for $b$" / "PSIS-LOO assigns the higher predictive score to the restricted candidate" — Pyro's `HalfNormal(5)` *is* `Normal(0,5)` truncated at 0, so the two priors are proportional (factor 2) on $b>0$ and the two posteriors are the **same distribution** wherever the free posterior has mass. From the artifact's own MLE (`results.json` `candidate_definition.observed_data_mle`: `b`=0.25128, `sigma`=0.32123) on `x=linspace(-10,10,20)` ($S_{xx}$=736.8), the observed-information posterior SD of $b$ is 0.0130, so the boundary is 19.4 SDs away and $P(b<0\mid y)\approx10^{-84}$; the same holds in every leave-one-out fold. Both candidates therefore estimate the identical `elpd_loo`, and the +0.413 gap is MCMC/PSIS estimation noise, not a constraint effect. The artifact carries the signature: the free fit has lower min bulk ESS (1004 vs 1638), higher `p_loo` (5.343 vs 5.169), and 15/20 pointwise differences favour the constrained fit by 0.02-0.05 (sum +0.629 vs -0.217) — the downward bias of log-of-a-noisier-mean, concentrated at the high-influence points. The paired SE 0.263 bounds data-level variability only, not MCMC error, so "0.413 vs SE 0.263" is not a valid guard, and the Pareto-$k$ caveat at :95-100 qualifies the wrong thing. **Fix:** replace :93-95 and :102-107 with the analytic statement (priors proportional on $b>0$; boundary 19 posterior SDs away; `elpd_loo` is the same population quantity for both candidates; the residual gap is estimator noise), and correct the reading — a null LOO difference *is* Haaf/Klaassen/Rouder's reported failure mode, not a departure from it, so ":102 does not reproduce a categorical LOO failure" must go. Amend the D63 "split null" / "direction favors the restriction" wording identically.

5. **[S3] [docs/paper-sie-jmp/05-case-C-nested-constraints.md:37-41; experiments/haaf_nested_constraint.py:260-292]** "The nesting check passed at a $2\times10^{-7}$ absolute tolerance … both candidates produced identical primary $G$ values" reports as a verification something the implementation enforces by construction. `free_candidates = free_vectors + constrained_vectors` and `constrained_candidates = constrained_vectors + [feasible free_vectors]` makes `constrained_candidates` ⊆ `free_candidates`, so `free_G <= constrained_G` holds by set inclusion and the `if free_G > constrained_G + G_ORDER_ATOL: raise` gate at :292 is unreachable; and when the free argmin is feasible, :285-290 re-evaluates that exact vector inside the constrained model and overwrites `constrained_G` with it, which is why `max_abs_gap_on_nonnegative_free_slope` is exactly `0.0` rather than a small float. **Fix:** describe the cross-seeded pools, state the ordering as an identity of the protocol ($M_r\subset M_e\Rightarrow\min_{M_r}G\ge\min_{M_e}G$), and drop "passed at a $2\times10^{-7}$ absolute tolerance", which describes a guard that cannot fire.

6. **[S3] [docs/paper-sie-jmp/05-case-C-nested-constraints.md:61-69, :106-107]** "does not support a claim that BMS* preferentially rewards a satisfied restriction **in this instance**" and "Here that event occurs once in 1,000 SIR predictives" frame a structurally forced outcome as data-dependent. Under the best-instance/soft-transfer protocol used here, $G_r(\psi)\ge G_e(\psi)$ for every $\psi$ because $M_r\subset M_e$, so the restricted candidate's aggregated probability can never exceed the free candidate's at any τ under either convention; only the *magnitude* of the gap is empirical. The acceptance criterion's "whichever way it comes out" is therefore one-sided by construction on the BMS* arm. **Fix:** state the one-sided bound before Table 5.1, then present the measured content as the size of the gap ($<10^{-5}$, from 1/1000 draws) and remove "in this instance"/"Here that event occurs".
